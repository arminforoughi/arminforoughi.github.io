    Method & data

    Terrain

    NASA LRO LOLA gridded elevation model LDEM_80S_80M (PDS, Mean Earth/Polar Axis frame), averaged to 160 m per pixel and covering everything south of 80°S. Everything you see — the 3D model, the shadows and the horizon profiles — comes from this grid.

    Horizon

    For each site, 1,440 rays are traced across the elevation model out to the edge of the map (up to ~430 km), on the true curved Moon. The highest terrain elevation angle is stored in 0.5° azimuth bins. Observer height = ground + the height you set.

    Sun & Earth

    Positions from Astronomy Engine (VSOP87 / ELP), rotated into the Moon-fixed frame with the IAU/WGCCRE lunar rotation model, including parallax from the site. Validated against JPL Horizons (DE441): 2,800 Sun and Earth positions at four polar sites over 2026–27 agree to within 0.004° in both azimuth and elevation.

    Definitions

    • Sunlight — time-averaged fraction of the solar disc above the terrain horizon (a vertical, Sun-tracking panel's relative power).
    • Sunlit / night — at least / less than half of the solar disc visible.
    • Earth in view — Earth's centre above the terrain horizon plus the minimum elevation you set. This is line of sight only, not a link budget.
    • Both — sunlight counted only while Earth is in view: the share of time a site has power and a direct link at once.

    Planning dates

    The Plan dates tab slides a mission of the chosen length across the analysis window. For each landing time it averages sunlight, Earth visibility and both together over the mission, and marks the best landing time for each site. With the touchdown rule on, only landing times that are sunlit with Earth in view are eligible.

    Sites elsewhere on the Moon

    The terrain model covers 80°S to the pole. Sites outside it, such as the Apollo sites, use the horizon of a smooth sphere, so local hills and crater walls are not included. They are here for comparison with the pole.

    Limits

    Features smaller than ~160 m (boulders, small craters, the lander itself) are not resolved, so short local shadows may be missed. Terrain north of 80°S is not included, which matters only for sites near the edge of the map. Results are for mission planning study and education, not flight operations.
